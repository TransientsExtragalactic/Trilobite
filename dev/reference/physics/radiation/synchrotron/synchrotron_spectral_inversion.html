Spectral Inversion#

See also

Methods: Single-Zone Synchrotron SEDs for the SED formalism and notation used throughout this page, and Synchrotron Spectral Energy Distributions for the SED classes and the closures they use.

The trilobite.radiation.synchrotron.spectral_inversion module provides pen-and-paper inversions of synchrotron spectra. Given a small number of observed spectral quantities, typically the epoch \(t\), a break frequency \(\nu_{\rm brk}\) and the flux \(F_{\rm brk}\) at that break, each inversion returns the physical parameters of a one-zone source, such as its magnetic field strength \(B\) and emitting radius \(R\), without building an SED model or running a fit.

These functions are intended as an easy analysis tool which sits outside of the SED generators in trilobite.radiation.synchrotron.SEDs. The SED classes derive a spectrum from physical parameters, and may be inverted through their own closures with the spectral regime detected for you. The inversions here instead take the opposite view:

  • they are closed-form (or, in one case, a short and robust numerical solve),

  • they assume the regime they are named after rather than detecting it, and

  • they return diagnostics so that the assumed regime can be checked after the fact.

The module is organized into families of inversions, each tied to a particular derivation in the literature and sharing a common set of conventions (see below). New families can be added without changing the others.

Overview#

Every inversion is a public, unit-aware function which accepts Quantity objects (or bare floats) and returns a dictionary of results. The simplest use takes the best-fit break of a broken power law and returns the magnetic field and radius:

import astropy.units as u
from trilobite.radiation.synchrotron.spectral_inversion import invert_ssa_Diggins26

out = invert_ssa_Diggins26(
    nu_brk=5 * u.GHz,
    F_brk=8 * u.mJy,
    distance=35 * u.Mpc,
    p=3.0,
    epsilon_E=0.1,
    epsilon_B=0.1,
)
print(out["B"], out["R"])

The remainder of this section describes the conventions shared by every inversion and how to choose between them.

Conventions#

Break frequency and flux. \(\nu_{\rm brk}\) is the best-fit break frequency of the spectrum. For a self-absorbed (SSA) break it is identified with the SSA frequency \(\nu_a\), which is accurate to a fraction of a percent across the relevant parameter space. \(F_{\rm brk}\) is the asymptotic intercept flux: the flux density at \(\nu_{\rm brk}\) of the optically thick asymptote of the fit, \(F_{\rm asym}\).

Important

The user must supply the correct asymptotic flux. If you fit a traditional (sharp) broken power law, the fitted normalization at the break is exactly this quantity. If you fit a smoothed broken power law, the flux at the break differs from the asymptotic intercept by a factor that depends on the smoothing, and the asymptotic flux must be extracted from the fit before it is passed to these functions.

Local, non-relativistic sources. The inversions assume a source in the local universe, so they take a single distance argument and do not apply a cosmological redshift correction. They also assume a non-relativistic outflow, so no Doppler or time-dilation correction is made. Unless you are working with relativistic sources such as gamma-ray bursts, this is perfectly serviceable. For relativistic sources these inversions should not be used.

Pitch angle. Two treatments are supported, matching the rest of Trilobite. By default a fixed pitch angle alpha is used (\(\pi/2\) unless specified). Setting pitch_average=True instead selects the isotropic coefficients \(c_5\) and \(c_6\), and, in the cooled inversions, the angle-averaged synchrotron loss rate.

Units. Inputs may be Quantity objects or bare floats. Bare floats are interpreted in CGS (Hz, \(\mathrm{erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}}\), cm, s and \(\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}\)), with the exception of invert_ssa_DeMarchi22(), which keeps the units of its original implementation (GHz, Jy and Mpc). Because the electron normalization uses the first moment of the distribution, p > 2 is required unless gamma_max is finite.

Broadcasting. Every observable (nu_brk, F_brk, distance, t, L) and every microphysical parameter (p, epsilon_E, epsilon_B, f_A, f_V, alpha, gamma_min, gamma_max) may be a scalar or an array, including a Quantity array. They are broadcast against one another following the usual numpy rules, and every array in a returned dictionary has the broadcast shape. A column of break frequencies and a row of electron indices therefore give a grid of solutions, and one luminosity per observation is simply an array alongside nu_brk and F_brk. This makes the inversions well suited to propagating posterior draws.

Return values. Each function returns a dictionary. All inversions return the magnetic field "B" and radius "R"; the cooled inversions additionally return the cooling Lorentz factor and a set of regime diagnostics (see Checking the Assumed Regime).

The Diggins et al. (2026) Inversions#

This family inverts an SSA break for a one-zone source with a power-law electron distribution whose normalization is set by equipartition with the magnetic field. The functions are labeled _Diggins26 and are derived in the appendix of Diggins et al. (2026). They differ in how the electrons at the break are treated:

  • invert_ssa_Diggins26() assumes uncooled electrons,

  • invert_ssa_synchrotron_cooled_Diggins26(), invert_ssa_ic_cooled_Diggins26() and invert_ssa_combined_cooled_Diggins26() assume cooled electrons, with the cooling Lorentz factor set self-consistently by the source age, and

  • invert_ssa_with_cooling_Diggins26() chooses between the cooled and uncooled solutions.

All of them share the same underlying forward model, summarized below.

Forward model and reduced notation

For an electron distribution \(N(\gamma) = N_0\gamma^{-q}\), the SSA frequency and the optically thick flux of a homogeneous sphere of radius \(R\) at distance \(D\) are

\[\nu_a = 2c_1\,(m_ec^2)^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q+4}}\left[c_6(q)\,f_V^{1/3}R\right]^{\frac{2}{q+4}} N_0^{\frac{2}{q+4}}\,(B\sin\alpha)^{\frac{q+2}{q+4}},\]
\[F_{\rm asym} = \frac{c_5(q)}{c_6(q)}\,\frac{\pi f_A R^2}{D^2}\,(B\sin\alpha)^{-1/2} \left(\frac{\nu_a}{2c_1}\right)^{5/2}.\]

The electron normalization is fixed by equipartition with the uncooled energy budget,

\[N_0 = A_N B^2, \qquad A_N = \frac{1}{8\pi m_ec^2}\,\frac{\epsilon_E}{\epsilon_B}\,\frac{1}{M_\gamma^{(1)}},\]

where \(M_\gamma^{(1)}\) is the first moment of the Lorentz-factor distribution for the index \(p\). Collecting the dependence on \(B\) and \(R\),

\[\nu_a = A_\nu\,A_N^{\frac{2}{q+4}}\,R^{\frac{2}{q+4}}B^{\frac{q+6}{q+4}}, \qquad F_{\rm asym} = A_F\,A_N^{\frac{5}{q+4}}\,D^{-2}\,(BR)^{\frac{2q+13}{q+4}},\]

with the reduced constants

\[A_\nu(q) = 2c_1\,(m_ec^2)^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q+4}}\left[c_6(q)\,f_V^{1/3}\right]^{\frac{2}{q+4}} \sin^{\frac{q+2}{q+4}}\alpha, \qquad A_F(q) = \frac{c_5(q)}{c_6(q)}\,\pi f_A\,\sin^{-1/2}\alpha\left(\frac{A_\nu(q)}{2c_1}\right)^{5/2}.\]

Uncooled Electrons#

invert_ssa_Diggins26() is the default choice when the electrons at the break have not had time to cool. With no cooling, the flux fixes the product \(BR\) and the break frequency then fixes \(B\), so the solution is a single closed-form expression. It needs nothing beyond the break, the distance and the microphysics.

out = invert_ssa_Diggins26(nu_brk, F_brk, distance, p=3.0, epsilon_E=0.1, epsilon_B=0.1)
out["B"], out["R"]
Derivation

For uncooled electrons \(q = p\), and the forward model above is inverted directly,

\[B = \frac{\nu_{\rm brk}}{A_\nu}\,A_F^{\frac{2}{2p+13}}\,A_N^{-\frac{4}{2p+13}}\, \left(F_{\rm brk}D^2\right)^{-\frac{2}{2p+13}}, \qquad R = \frac{A_\nu}{\nu_{\rm brk}}\,A_F^{-\frac{p+6}{2p+13}}\,A_N^{-\frac{1}{2p+13}}\, \left(F_{\rm brk}D^2\right)^{\frac{p+6}{2p+13}}.\]

Cooled Electrons#

If the electrons at the break have cooled, the normalization of the emitting segment is reduced, and the inferred \(B\) and \(R\) change. Three inversions are provided, which differ only in what sets the cooling Lorentz factor \(\gamma_c\):

  • invert_ssa_synchrotron_cooled_Diggins26(): synchrotron losses, \(\gamma_c = A_c/(B^2 t)\),

  • invert_ssa_ic_cooled_Diggins26(): inverse-Compton losses on the radiation field of a central source of luminosity \(L\), \(\gamma_c = A_{\rm IC}R^2/(Lt)\), and

  • invert_ssa_combined_cooled_Diggins26(): both, with the loss rates adding.

All three require the epoch t, which is treated as the effective cooling time of the source. The IC-cooled and combined inversions additionally require the seed luminosity L at that epoch. They are valid when the cooling break lies well below the SSA break, \(\nu_c \ll \nu_a\), in the slow-cooling regime \(\gamma_c > \gamma_m\); whether this holds for your solution is reported through the diagnostics described in Checking the Assumed Regime.

out = invert_ssa_synchrotron_cooled_Diggins26(
    nu_brk, F_brk, distance, t, p=3.0, epsilon_E=0.1, epsilon_B=0.1
)
out["B"], out["R"], out["gamma_c"], out["nu_c_over_nu_a"]

Important

For isotropic pitch angles (pitch_average=True) the synchrotron cooling constant is \(9\pi m_e c/\sigma_T\), from the angle-averaged loss rate \(\langle\sin^2\alpha\rangle = 2/3\). This is not the \(\sin\alpha\to1\) limit of the fixed-angle constant, \(6\pi m_e c/(\sigma_T\sin^2\alpha)\). It assumes that pitch-angle scattering is fast compared with cooling.

Cooled forward model

In the slow-cooling regime \(\gamma_m < \gamma_c\), continuous injection and radiative losses give

\[\begin{split}N(\gamma) = N_0 \begin{cases} \gamma^{-p}, & \gamma_m \le \gamma < \gamma_c,\\ \gamma_c\,\gamma^{-(p+1)}, & \gamma_c \le \gamma \le \gamma_{\rm max}. \end{cases}\end{split}\]

For \(\nu_c \ll \nu_a \ll \nu_{\rm max}\), the emission and absorption near \(\nu_a\) come from the cooled segment, so the forward model above applies with

\[p \to p+1, \qquad N_0 \to N_0\gamma_c .\]

The normalization \(N_0 = A_NB^2\) is still set by the uncooled energy budget, which overestimates the electron energy by a factor \(1/(1-\delta_c)\), with \(\delta_c = (\gamma_c/\gamma_m)^{2-p}/(p-1)\) for \(\gamma_{\rm max}\to\infty\) and \(p>2\). This is returned as delta_c. We write \(A_\nu^{(c)} = A_\nu(p+1)\) and \(A_F^{(c)} = A_F(p+1)\) below.

Derivation: synchrotron cooling

With \(\gamma_c = A_c/(B^2 t)\), the factor \(B^2\) in \(N_0\) cancels the \(B^{-2}\) in \(\gamma_c\), so \(N_0\gamma_c = A_NA_c/t \equiv \Lambda\) is independent of \(B\) and \(R\). The system is then a pair of power laws,

\[B = \left(\frac{\nu_{\rm brk}}{A_\nu^{(c)}}\right)^{\frac{2p+15}{2p+7}} \left(\frac{F_{\rm brk}D^2}{A_F^{(c)}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{2p+7}}\Lambda^{-\frac{4}{2p+7}}, \qquad R = \left(\frac{F_{\rm brk}D^2}{A_F^{(c)}}\right)^{\frac{p+3}{2p+7}} \left(\frac{\nu_{\rm brk}}{A_\nu^{(c)}}\right)^{-\frac{2p+5}{2p+7}}\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2p+7}},\]

with the synchrotron cooling constant

\[\begin{split}A_c = \begin{cases} \dfrac{6\pi m_e c}{\sigma_T\sin^2\alpha} & \text{fixed pitch angle},\\[2ex] \dfrac{9\pi m_e c}{\sigma_T} & \text{pitch averaged}. \end{cases}\end{split}\]
Derivation: inverse-Compton cooling

In the Thomson regime, an electron in the radiation field of a central source of luminosity \(L\) has \(\gamma_c = A_{\rm IC}R^2/(Lt)\) with \(A_{\rm IC} = 3\pi m_ec^2/\sigma_T\). This loss rate is independent of the pitch angle. Now \(\gamma_c\propto R^2\), so there is no cancellation against the normalization,

\[N_0\gamma_c = \Lambda_{\rm IC}\,(BR)^2, \qquad \Lambda_{\rm IC} = \frac{A_NA_{\rm IC}}{L\,t},\]

but the system remains log-linear and can be solved in closed form,

\[B = \left(\frac{\nu_{\rm brk}}{A_\nu^{(c)}}\right)^{\frac{2p+25}{2p+17}} \left(\frac{F_{\rm brk}D^2}{A_F^{(c)}}\right)^{-\frac{6}{2p+17}}\Lambda_{\rm IC}^{-\frac{4}{2p+17}}, \qquad R = \left(\frac{F_{\rm brk}D^2}{A_F^{(c)}}\right)^{\frac{p+7}{2p+17}} \left(\frac{\nu_{\rm brk}}{A_\nu^{(c)}}\right)^{-\frac{2p+15}{2p+17}}\Lambda_{\rm IC}^{-\frac{1}{2p+17}}.\]
Derivation: combined cooling

With both loss channels the rates add,

\[\gamma_c = \frac{A_c}{B^2t}\,\frac{1}{1+Y}, \qquad Y \equiv \frac{A_cL}{A_{\rm IC}R^2B^2},\]

and \(\gamma_c\) is a sum of two power laws, so there is no closed form. The optically thick asymptote does not involve \(\gamma_c\), and gives \(R(B)\propto B^{1/4}\). Substituting this and \(\gamma_c(B, R(B))\) into the SSA condition leaves a single equation \(g(\ln B) = 0\), whose slope

\[\frac{dg}{d\ln B} = \frac{2p+17}{4} - \frac{5}{2}\,\frac{1}{1+Y} \in \left[\frac{2p+7}{4},\ \frac{2p+17}{4}\right]\]

is bounded and strictly positive, so the root is unique. Since \(Y\propto B^{-5/2}\) along \(R(B)\), \(g\) is also concave, and Newton iterations started below the root approach it monotonically.

Adding a second loss channel can only lower \(\gamma_c\) at fixed \((B, R)\), so each single-channel closed form is a lower bound on \(B\). Starting from the larger of the two, the minimum slope gives an upper bound, and the root is bracketed analytically. The bracket guards a Newton iteration which converges in a few steps and is vectorized over inputs. A luminosity of zero removes the IC channel and recovers the synchrotron-cooled result.

Checking the Assumed Regime#

The cooled inversions assume \(\nu_c \ll \nu_a\) and \(\gamma_c > \gamma_m\), where the characteristic frequency of the cooling electrons is \(\nu_c = c_{1,\gamma}B\sin\alpha\,\gamma_c^2\). Because the regime is assumed rather than detected, each cooled inversion returns diagnostics for checking it:

  • nu_c_over_nu_a: the ratio \(\nu_c/\nu_a\), which should be much smaller than one,

  • consistent: whether \(\nu_c < \nu_a\),

  • accurate: whether \(\nu_c < 0.1\,\nu_a\), for which the cooled asymptotic forms are good to better than 0.1%,

  • slow_cooling: whether \(\gamma_c > \gamma_{\min}\),

  • delta_c: the fractional energy error of the uncooled normalization (NaN unless \(p>2\) and gamma_max is infinite), and

  • for the IC-cooled and combined inversions, the Compton parameter Y and ic_dominated (\(Y>1\)).

A warning is also raised if \(\nu_c \ge \nu_a\) for any input, since the results are then not self-consistent.

Choosing Between Cooled and Uncooled#

Often you do not know in advance whether the electrons at the break are cooled. invert_ssa_with_cooling_Diggins26() evaluates both solutions and keeps the one which is consistent with its own assumption. The luminosity L is treated as data, with one value per observation, alongside t, nu_brk and F_brk; use L=0 if there is no seed radiation field.

out = invert_ssa_with_cooling_Diggins26(
    nu_brk, F_brk, distance, t, L=1e42 * u.erg / u.s, p=3.0, epsilon_E=0.1, epsilon_B=0.1
)
out["branch"], out["B"], out["R"]

For each observation, the function proceeds as follows:

  1. The uncooled solution is evaluated, and the cooling break it implies is computed with both loss channels. It is accepted if \(\nu_c > \nu_a\).

  2. The cooled (combined) solution is evaluated. It is accepted if \(\nu_c < \nu_a\) and \(\gamma_c > \gamma_{\min}\).

  3. If exactly one is accepted, that branch is returned and labeled "uncooled" or "cooled".

Observations for which neither or both are accepted cannot be assigned to a branch, and are labeled:

  • "fast_cooling": the cooled ordering holds but \(\gamma_c \le \gamma_{\min}\), a regime that the slow-cooling distribution does not describe, or

  • "ambiguous": both or neither branch is consistent, typically because \(\nu_c\approx\nu_a\), where the asymptotic forms fail.

These observations return NaN for \(B\), \(R\) and \(\gamma_c\). The full solutions of both branches are returned under "cooled" and "uncooled" for inspection.

The De Marchi et al. (2022) Inversion#

invert_ssa_DeMarchi22() implements the analytic SSA inversion of DeMarchi et al.[1] for uncooled electrons. It uses the same nu_brk and F_brk as the other inversions and is useful for comparing directly with results in the literature.

Warning

The De Marchi inversion does not pitch-angle average. It uses the fixed pitch angle theta throughout. To use the isotropic pitch-angle coefficients, use invert_ssa_Diggins26() with pitch_average=True.

Its interface follows the original implementation more closely than the rest of the module: bare floats are in GHz, Jy and Mpc, the filling factor is called f, and the singular case \(p = 2\) is not supported.

out = invert_ssa_DeMarchi22(5 * u.GHz, 8 * u.mJy, 35 * u.Mpc, p=3.0, f=0.5)
out["B"], out["R"]

API Reference#

invert_ssa_Diggins26

Invert an SSA break for \(B\) and \(R\) assuming uncooled electrons.

invert_ssa_synchrotron_cooled_Diggins26

Invert an SSA break for \(B\) and \(R\) assuming synchrotron-cooled electrons.

invert_ssa_ic_cooled_Diggins26

Invert an SSA break for \(B\) and \(R\) assuming inverse-Compton-cooled electrons.

invert_ssa_combined_cooled_Diggins26

Invert an SSA break assuming electrons cooled by both synchrotron and inverse-Compton losses.

invert_ssa_with_cooling_Diggins26

Invert an SSA break, selecting between the cooled and uncooled branches by self-consistency.

invert_ssa_DeMarchi22

Invert an SSA break using the analytic formulae of DeMarchi et al.[1].
